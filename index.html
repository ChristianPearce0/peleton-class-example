🔑 Key Takeaways — Grounded in the FY2025 10-K

📈
Churn Is Accelerating
Average Net Monthly CF Subscription Churn rose to 1.6% in FY2025, up from 1.4% in FY2024 and 1.3% in FY2023. This means Peloton is losing a larger share of its subscriber base each year — the first condition for a demand-driven death spiral. App churn at 7.0%/month is even more severe.
🏗️
Fixed Costs Are Sticky
Subscription fixed costs — instructors, studio rent, D&A ($28.5M), and stock-based comp ($36.3M) — totaled approximately $452.3M in FY2025. These costs barely moved despite a $35M drop in subscription revenue year-over-year. As subs fall, each remaining subscriber must absorb a larger share of these fixed costs.
💸
Revenue Sensitivity Is High
Subscription revenue fell from $1,708.7M (FY2024) to $1,673.7M (FY2025) — a 2.1% decline driven by fewer paid subs. At the current revenue-per-subscriber of ~$499/year, every 100,000 subscribers lost equals ~$50M in annual revenue. The fixed cost base means that revenue loss flows almost directly to gross profit erosion.
🔄
The Spiral Mechanism
As subscriber count declines: (1) Revenue falls proportionally. (2) Fixed costs remain near-constant. (3) Fixed cost per subscriber rises. (4) Peloton may need to raise prices or cut content quality to maintain margins. (5) Higher prices or lower quality drive further churn. This is a textbook demand-driven death spiral — the same dynamic as the telecom example in the course slides.
🛡️
Current Margin Provides a Buffer
Subscription Gross Margin of 69.1% ($1,157.1M gross profit) provides significant headroom before the spiral becomes existential. The 10-K notes a revolving credit covenant requiring subscription revenue of at least $1.2B on a trailing four-quarter basis — a floor that constrains how far the spiral can go before triggering a debt crisis.
⚙️
Restructuring as a Counter-Spiral Tool
Peloton's 2022, 2024, and 2025 Restructuring Plans are designed to reduce the fixed cost base — the key lever to break the spiral. The 2025 plan targets ≥$100M in run-rate savings by end of FY2026. If successful, this lowers the breakeven subscriber count and reduces the spiral's severity. However, three consecutive restructurings signal execution risk.
✅
FY2025: Spiral Slowing, Not Stopped
Net loss improved dramatically from $(551.9M) in FY2024 to $(118.9M) in FY2025, and Adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $403.6M. Free Cash Flow was $323.7M. These improvements reflect cost-cutting, not subscriber growth. The subscriber base still declined from 3,597,697 (FY2024) to 3,352,394 (FY2025) — a loss of ~245,000 paid subs.
⚠️
Practical Capacity Rate as a Solution
As the course slides note, using a practical capacity overhead rate (based on maximum subscriber capacity, not actual) would separate the "cost of unused capacity" from the cost per active subscriber. This prevents the death spiral by keeping per-unit costs stable regardless of volume — but requires Peloton to explicitly recognize and manage its excess content/studio capacity as a separate cost line.
